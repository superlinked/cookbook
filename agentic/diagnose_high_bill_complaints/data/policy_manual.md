# Alder Valley Water: Customer Service Rules and Rates

## 1.1 Purpose of these rules

These rules set out how Alder Valley Water measures, bills and collects for water service. Where a rule refers to a Schedule, the Schedule at the end of these rules forms part of that rule.

## 1.2 Definitions: billing period and customer of record

A billing period is one calendar month. The customer of record is the person or business named on the service agreement for an address, and stays responsible for all charges at that address until service is closed under section 8.1.

## 2.1 Meter types

Automated meters report hourly use to the utility. Manually read meters are read on site by a field technician once a month and do not report hourly use.

## 2.2 Access to the meter

The customer must keep the meter reachable. A locked gate, fence, vehicle or animal that stops a technician reaching the meter is an access problem, and the utility will leave a door notice asking for access.

## 3.1 Estimated reads

When a manually read meter can't be reached, the utility issues an Estimated Read in place of an actual reading and marks the bill as estimated. An Estimated Read is a calculated figure and may be higher or lower than actual use.

## 3.2 Correcting an Estimated Read

When an actual reading becomes available after one or more Estimated Reads, the utility rebills the estimated periods. The actual volume used since the last actual reading is divided evenly across those periods, each period is recharged at the rates in force on its first day, and the difference is applied to the account as a credit or a charge.

## 3.3 Customer-supplied readings

The utility accepts a Customer-Supplied Reading as an actual reading for section 3.2 if the customer sends a dated photograph showing both the meter register and the meter serial number, and the serial number matches the one on the account. A photograph with a different serial number or an unreadable register is not accepted, and a technician visit is scheduled within 10 business days.

## 3.4 Limits on rebilling

Rebilling under section 3.2 can go back no more than 12 billing periods. A credit over the amount in Schedule B needs a supervisor's approval before it is applied.

## 4.1 How charges are calculated

Each bill is a fixed Base Charge plus a volume charge. The volume charge is worked out in tiers from Schedule A, and the rates that apply are the ones in force on the first day of the billing period.

## 4.2 Rate changes

Rates are set by the utility's board and take effect on the date in Schedule A. A rate change isn't grounds for an adjustment.

## 5.1 Reviewing a high bill

Staff reviewing a high bill should first check whether the bill rests on an actual reading or an Estimated Read, then compare use with earlier periods, then look at hourly use if the meter reports it.

## 5.2 Signs of continuous flow

On an automated meter, use that does not fall to zero between 1 a.m. and 5 a.m. for three or more nights in a row is Continuous Flow. Continuous Flow is often a leak, but it can also be a device left running, and the data alone doesn't show which.

## 5.3 Short periods of heavy use

A short period of heavy use that then stops can have many causes, including a repaired leak, filling a pool or an irrigation system. Hourly use doesn't show which it was, and staff should ask the customer what happened on those dates and shouldn't state a cause.

## 6.1 Responsibility for private plumbing

The customer is responsible for all pipes and fixtures after the meter, and for water that passes through the meter, whether or not it was used on purpose.

## 6.2 Excess Use Adjustment

The utility may reduce a bill raised by a sudden failure of private plumbing through an Excess Use Adjustment. The customer must send a paid repair invoice from a licensed plumber dated within 60 days of the bill. Eligibility limits are in Schedule B.

## 6.3 Amount of an Excess Use Adjustment

An Excess Use Adjustment credits 50 percent of the volume charge on the volume above the customer's average use over the previous 12 billing periods. Where fewer than 12 periods exist, the available periods are used.

## 6.4 What an Excess Use Adjustment doesn't cover

No Excess Use Adjustment is available for irrigation, filling a pool, a fixture left running, or leaks the customer knew of and didn't repair within 30 days.

## 7.1 Meter Accuracy Test

A customer may request a Meter Accuracy Test. The fee is in Schedule B and is refunded if the meter is found to register more than 1.5 percent fast. If it does, the utility rebills up to the previous 12 billing periods.

## 7.2 Meter faults

A meter fault is rare, and a meter that registers wrongly more often registers slow than fast. Staff shouldn't tell a customer the meter is accurate without a Meter Accuracy Test.

## 8.1 Closing service

To close service, the customer of record must ask the utility to close the account and give a forwarding address. Moving out doesn't close service. Charges continue until the date the utility receives the request, or a later date the customer asks for.

## 8.2 Backdating a closure

A closure can be backdated to the move-out date only if the new occupant or owner has opened service from that date.

## 9.1 Installment Agreements

A residential customer with a balance over the amount in Schedule B may ask for an Installment Agreement to pay the balance over up to 6 billing periods, with no interest. Current charges must be paid in full each period while the agreement runs. Only one agreement can be open at a time.

## 9.2 Late fees and shutoff

A late fee in Schedule B applies to a balance unpaid 30 days after the bill date. Service isn't shut off while an Installment Agreement is being kept, or while a bill is under review under section 5.1.

## 9.3 Customer Assistance Program

Residential customers whose household income is at or below the limit in Schedule C can apply to the Customer Assistance Program for a discount on the Base Charge and on the first tier of the volume charge. The discount starts with the first bill after approval and isn't applied to past bills.

## 9.4 Medical hardship

A customer with a medical certificate from a licensed physician can ask for a 60 day hold on shutoff. The hold doesn't reduce what is owed.

## Schedule A: Rates

From 1 January 2026: Base Charge $18.00 per billing period. Tier 1, up to 6,000 gallons: $3.20 per 1,000 gallons. Tier 2, over 6,000 and up to 15,000 gallons: $4.80 per 1,000 gallons. Tier 3, over 15,000 gallons: $7.40 per 1,000 gallons.

From 1 July 2026: Base Charge $19.50 per billing period. Tier 1, up to 6,000 gallons: $3.50 per 1,000 gallons. Tier 2, over 6,000 and up to 15,000 gallons: $5.20 per 1,000 gallons. Tier 3, over 15,000 gallons: $8.10 per 1,000 gallons.

## Schedule B: Limits and fees

Excess Use Adjustment: no more than one in any 24 month period, counted from the date of the last adjustment. Credits above $250 need a supervisor's approval. Meter Accuracy Test fee: $45. Installment Agreement: available on balances over $100. Late fee: 1.5 percent of the unpaid balance.

## Schedule C: Customer Assistance Program

Household income limit: 60 percent of the area median income, shown on the utility's application form. Discount: 50 percent of the Base Charge and 25 percent of the Tier 1 volume charge. Approval lasts 12 months and can be renewed.
