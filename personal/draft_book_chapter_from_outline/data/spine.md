# Chapter 6: Who owns the words?

## Why I draft with open models

- The big chat services say I own what their models write for me, but their terms stop me using it
  to train another model.
- I want to fine-tune a small model on my own drafts one day, so I don't want that clause on them.
- Open-weight models under Apache 2.0 put no conditions on what I do with the output.

## Watermarks in the text

- A text watermark works by nudging which word the model picks next, so it lives in the word
  choice itself.
- Google has watermarked Gemini's text with SynthID, and Anthropic said in 2026 that Claude would
  watermark all of its output.
- The EU AI Act requires providers to mark AI-generated content in a way a machine can detect.
- An open model served without a watermark leaves it to me to say where AI helped.

## What copyright protects

- In the US, only work by a human author can be copyrighted.
- Typing a prompt doesn't make me the author of what comes back.
- How I select and arrange the material, and what I rewrite myself, can still be protected.
- Most publishers now make authors sign that no AI wrote any part of the book.

## Keeping a record

- I keep a record of which sentences a model drafted and which I wrote.
- Every claim in the chapter should point to a source a reader can check.
- The draft is a starting point: I rewrite every paragraph before it goes in the book.
