# Customer due diligence handbook: personal accounts

Written by the compliance team for KYC analysts. This is a made-up handbook for a demo, not legal
advice and not any bank's real procedure. The checks the onboarding workflow runs are in
policy.yaml; this handbook explains them, and covers the cases the workflow doesn't.

## 1.1 What this handbook covers

This handbook covers customer due diligence for personal current accounts: what the application
form must hold, which documents prove identity and address, and what to do when a customer can't
provide the usual documents. Business accounts follow the business handbook. Where this handbook
and the automated KYC check disagree, the analyst decides, and records why in a case note.

## 2.1 The application form

Every personal application must give the customer's surname, given names, date of birth, full
address (house number, street, town and postcode, and the flat number where there is one),
nationality, occupation and country of tax residence. A field holding only "n/a", "-", "tbc" or
"unknown" counts as blank. Ask the customer for any blank field before the account is opened.

## 2.2 Identity documents

We accept a passport, a national identity card or a driving licence. The document must be in
date on the day the case is received. For a passport or an identity card, the check digits in the
machine-readable lines must hold. The name and date of birth must be the ones on the form.

## 3.1 Accepted proof of address

We accept a utility bill (energy or water), a bank statement or a council tax bill. It must be in
the customer's name, show the address on the form, and be a separate document from the one that
proves identity.

## 3.2 What isn't accepted as proof of address

We don't accept a mobile phone bill as proof of address. A mobile contract follows the customer,
not the property, and anyone can have a bill sent to any address. We also don't accept a credit
card statement from another lender, a payslip, or a letter addressed to "the occupier". The issuer
decides the type, not what the customer called the document when they uploaded it.

## 3.3 How recent

A proof of address must be dated within three months before the case was received, and not after
it.

## 4.1 Customers who moved in the last three months

A customer who moved in the last three months often has no bill at the new address yet: the
energy account may still be in the landlord's or the previous owner's name, and the first bill
can take weeks. This section applies when the address on the form is new and the customer can't
provide a document from section 3.1 at it.

## 4.2 What we accept instead, with sign-off

Instead of a document from section 3.1, we accept a signed tenancy agreement or a solicitor's
completion letter for the new address, dated within the last three months and in the customer's
name. The analyst must sign it off in the case notes before the account is opened. A mobile phone
bill still doesn't count (section 3.2). The automated KYC check doesn't know this exception and
will show the proof of address as missing; the analyst's sign-off is the record that it was
accepted.

## 4.3 The follow-up document within 90 days

An account opened under section 4.2 needs a utility bill, bank statement or council tax bill at
the new address within 90 days of opening. Tell the customer this when the account is opened, and
chase it at 60 days. If it doesn't arrive, restrict the account and refer it to the team lead.

## 5.1 Exceptions and who approves them

Any other exception to sections 2 and 3 must be approved by a team lead, never by the analyst
alone. A request for an exception is not an approval. The team lead records the approval, the
reason and any follow-up in a case note.

## 5.2 Case notes

Write a case note for every decision that isn't the automated check's: an exception, a sign-off
under section 4.2, a document requested or received, a restriction or a closure. Say what was
decided, by whom, and why.

## 6.1 Changes to this handbook

1 July 2025: country of tax residence became a mandatory form field for personal accounts
(section 2.1). Cases received before that date may have it blank; that was not a gap at the time.
The automated KYC check always checks against the policy as it is today.
