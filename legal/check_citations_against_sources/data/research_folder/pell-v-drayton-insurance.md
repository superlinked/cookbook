# Pell v. Drayton Insurance Agency

141 Westm. App. 90 (2008)
Westmark Court of Appeals

## Facts

Joanna Pell sold commercial insurance for the Drayton Insurance Agency for eleven years. Her employment agreement barred her, for one year after leaving, from soliciting any client the agency served during her last two years there. It did not stop her from working for a competitor or from selling insurance to anyone else. She joined a competing agency and, within a month, wrote policies for fourteen Drayton clients.

Drayton sued for breach. Pell argued that the agreement was an unreasonable covenant not to compete. The trial court granted summary judgment to Drayton.

## Majority

A covenant not to compete keeps a former employee out of a line of work. Pell's agreement did not. She was free to sell insurance the day she left, to anyone but the clients Drayton had paid her to serve. A nonsolicitation agreement limited to the employer's existing clients is not a covenant not to compete and is reviewed only for reasonableness of its duration.

One year is reasonable. It gives the agency time to introduce a new agent to its clients and gives the clients time to decide whom they want to work with. We do not reach whether a longer period could be justified on another record.

Affirmed.

## Dissent

Judge Varga, dissenting. I agree that the agreement is a nonsolicitation agreement, but I would not stop at one year. An employer's long-standing client relationships justify a restriction of up to three years, because a commercial client reviews its coverage only once a year and the relationship an agent built over a decade does not fade in a single renewal cycle.

The majority's narrow holding invites the next agency to draft for one year and no more, whatever its clients look like. I would hold that a court should measure the permitted period by the renewal cycle of the business, and I respectfully dissent from the suggestion that one year is the outer limit.
