# Carrow Builders, Inc. v. Ellery Holdings

135 Westm. 2d 260 (1998)
Westmark Supreme Court

## Facts

Ellery Holdings owned a six-story office building. Eight years after Carrow Builders finished it, the exterior cladding began to fail. No one was hurt and no other property was damaged. Ellery sued Carrow in negligence for the cost of replacing the cladding; the contract claim was time-barred.

## Majority

Tort law protects people and property from physical harm. Contract law protects the expectations parties bargain for. When a building is defective but has hurt no one, the owner has lost the benefit of its bargain, and the law of contract governs. The economic loss rule bars a negligence claim for purely economic loss arising from a defective building; the owner's remedy lies in contract.

Ellery could have bargained for a longer warranty or for indemnity. It did not, and it may not use tort law to get the protection it did not buy. Reversed with instructions to dismiss.
