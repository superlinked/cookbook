# Fennimore v. Stoke Constructors

221 Westm. 2d 410 (2020)
Westmark Supreme Court

## Facts

Fennimore owned a commercial warehouse whose roof began to sag because of a design error by Stoke Constructors' engineer. No person and no other property was harmed. Fennimore sued in negligence. Relying on Carrow Builders, the trial court dismissed.

## Majority

Carrow Builders treated every claim for economic loss as a contract claim. In the two decades since, that rule has barred claims against professionals whose duties do not come from the contract at all. We now overrule Carrow Builders.

A negligence claim for purely economic loss may proceed where the defendant owed a duty of care independent of the contract; the economic loss rule no longer bars such a claim in Westmark. Whether an independent duty exists turns on the defendant's profession and the risk of harm. A licensed engineer owes a duty of reasonable care in design that the law imposes whatever the contract says.

Reversed and remanded.

## Dissent

Justice Pryce, dissenting. Carrow Builders gave commercial parties a clear rule for twenty-two years: what you did not bargain for, you do not get. The majority trades that rule for a case-by-case search for independent duties. I would adhere to Carrow Builders and affirm.
