# Kessler Tools, Inc. v. Marrow

88 Ard. App. 412 (2020)
Ardena Court of Appeals

## Facts

Paul Marrow, a machinist, signed a covenant not to compete for three years anywhere in Ardena as a condition of entering Kessler Tools' paid apprenticeship. Kessler spent about forty thousand dollars training him. Eight months after finishing the apprenticeship he left for a competing shop across the street.

## Majority

Marrow asks us to adopt a reasonableness test that weighs the employee's position and the breadth of the restraint. That is not Ardena law. Under Ardena law, a covenant not to compete ancillary to employment is enforceable as written if it protects the employer's investment in training, without regard to the employee's position.

Marrow also asks that, if the covenant is enforceable, we narrow it to the county where he works. Ardena courts do not narrow overbroad covenants; they enforce or strike them as a whole. The legislature has not given courts a power to reform these agreements, and we decline to take one. The covenant protects Kessler's training investment and is enforced as written.

Affirmed.
