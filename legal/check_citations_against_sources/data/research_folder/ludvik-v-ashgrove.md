# Ludvik v. Ashgrove

302 Ard. 588 (2012)
Ardena Supreme Court

## Facts

The Ashgroves held a birthday party for their adult son. A guest drank wine and beer over several hours, drove home and injured Ludvik in a collision. Ludvik sued the Ashgroves. The trial court granted summary judgment to the hosts.

## Majority

Ludvik asks us to recognize a duty that Ardena has never imposed. Whether to create a new form of liability for private hosts involves choices about insurance, household conduct and the reach of the criminal law that a court is poorly placed to make on the record of one case.

An Ardena social host owes no duty to third parties for injuries caused by an adult guest to whom the host served alcohol; whether to impose such liability is a question for the legislature. The legislature has acted on commercial sellers in the Dram Shop Act and has chosen not to reach private hosts.

Affirmed.

## Dissent

Justice Orme, dissenting. Courts made the common law of negligence, and the duty of care is ours to define. Leaving it to the legislature is a choice too, and here it leaves the injured third party to bear the whole loss. I would recognize a narrow duty where a host keeps serving a guest the host knows is visibly intoxicated and about to drive.
